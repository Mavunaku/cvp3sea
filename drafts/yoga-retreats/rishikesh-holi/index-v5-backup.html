The Experience

Not just a trip to India

A deep immersion into the wisdom of Vedanta — in a place where spiritual life is part of every single day.

“Who am I, really?”

Vedanta — the knowledge of our true nature

Not my body, thoughts, emotions or roles — who am I beyond all of this? A daily Vedanta course turns attention inward, toward what stays unchanged while the outer world changes: a path to inner freedom, peace and steadiness.

  • Daily Vedanta course, plus satsang — open spiritual conversation
  • See your thoughts, emotions and life patterns in a new way
  • Find inner support that doesn’t depend on circumstances

Practice that settles the nervous system

Sunrise Hatha Yoga, pranayama and meditation — then the healing power of sound, and Shakti practices in the evenings.

  • Hatha Yoga, pranayama & meditation every morning
  • Nada Yoga with Margarita — professional singer, student of Gurnimit Singh
  • Sound healing with an invited Indian master
  • Shakti practices: sacred mantras & intuitive dance

Live inside Parmarth Niketan

A sacred ashram on the banks of the Ganga, at the foot of the Himalayas — where ritual is part of the day, not an excursion.

  • Sacred Yagya each morning, Ganga Aarti each evening at 5:00 PM
  • A first ritual dip in the Ganges on arrival day
  • Holi, celebrated with the ashram’s students, teachers and the Guru

Simple, sattvic, easy to find

Rishikesh is full of light, vegetarian sattvic cafés. We share group lunches at a restaurant, and your host and translator help you find nourishing choices every day.

  • Group lunch at a restaurant on most days
  • A festive Holi thali on March 22
  • Meals are not included in the package price
Day 6 · Rangwali Holi

A sacred morning, then a burst of color

The day opens with yoga and a sacred yagya. At 11 AM the ashram comes alive with color — Holi celebrated with the Rishi Kumars (students of the ashram), the Acharyas (teachers) and the Guru. A festival of love, joy, unity and the triumph of light.

    Cancellation & refundsThe $500 deposit is non-refundable; the rest is refundable until 45 days before.

    The $500 deposit per person is non-refundable once paid. The balance is due 45 days before departure; if you cancel before then, what you’ve paid beyond the deposit is refundable. After that, payment becomes non-refundable — which is why we recommend trip-cancellation insurance.

    Do I need a visa?Most travelers do — an e-Visa can be applied for online.

    Most travelers do — many nationalities can apply for an e-Visa online. Requirements vary, so check with your nearest Indian consulate or the official e-Visa portal early. It’s your responsibility as a guest; we’re happy to point you to resources.

    What level of yoga do I need?All levels are welcome, beginners included.

    All levels are welcome, including complete beginners — poses can be modified. Days start early (Hatha Yoga at 6:00 AM) and the schedule is full, so some stamina for long sitting and gentle movement helps.

    Traveling solo?Very welcome — share a room, or take one of your own.

    Solo travelers are very welcome. The $2,000 package is a shared three-person room at the ashram with fellow guests. For a room of your own, a private single room at a hotel next to the ashram is +$1,000, for $3,000 in total. Note that this room is not in the ashram — the hotel is outside the ashram grounds.

    Which airport?Delhi (4.5 h drive) or Dehradun (1 h drive).

    Delhi (DEL) has the most flights — about 4.5 hours’ drive to Rishikesh. Dehradun (DED) is closer — about 1 hour — with fewer flights. Open Rishikesh in Maps.

    Food & dietary needsMeals aren’t included — many light sattvic cafés nearby.

    Meals aren’t included. The area has many light, vegetarian sattvic cafés, and we share group lunches at a restaurant. Tell us about any dietary needs and your host and translator will help you find easy options.

    What to pack & the weatherMarch is mild and dry — warm days, cooler evenings by the river.

    March is mild and dry — warm days (70s–80s°F / low-to-mid 20s°C), cooler mornings and evenings by the river. Pack light layers, modest clothing for the ashram (shoulders and knees covered), comfortable walking shoes, and an outfit you don’t mind staining for Holi.
