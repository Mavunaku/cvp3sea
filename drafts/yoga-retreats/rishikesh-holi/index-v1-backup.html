About This Retreat

More Than a Journey to India

This won’t be just a trip to India, or just an introduction to yoga and spiritual practices. I want to guide you through a deep immersion into the wisdom of Vedanta — one of the most important spiritual and philosophical teachings of India.

The central question of Vedanta
Who am I, really?

Not my body, not my thoughts, not my emotions, not my social roles — and not even the personality I’m used to identifying with. Who am I beyond all of this?

VEDANTA is the ancient knowledge of our true nature — of the soul, of God, and of the nature of all existence. It invites us to explore the most important question of human life.

This knowledge makes life considerably easier. Very often we look for support in the outer world, trying to find happiness through relationships, achievements, recognition, and material stability. But Vedanta turns our attention inward — toward understanding our true nature, and recognizing what in us stays unchanged despite all the changes of the outer world.

It is a path to inner freedom, harmony, peace, and a deeper understanding of life.

During the retreat, a true spiritual immersion awaits us in the sacred atmosphere of the Parmarth Niketan ashram, on the banks of the Ganga at the foot of the Himalayas. We won’t only study philosophy — we’ll join knowledge with practice.

Nada Yoga — the Yoga of Sound

Nada Yoga is an ancient practice that uses sound, voice, mantras, and attentive listening as tools to explore our inner space and deepen meditation.

In the Indian tradition, sound is seen not only as a physical phenomenon but as a subtle manifestation of energy — a way to calm the mind, develop concentration, release inner tension, and turn attention from the outer world toward inner silence.

With Margarita — professional singer & student of Gurnimit Singh, the finest Nada Yoga teacher

I want to create a space where you can:

Most importantly — to take home not just new knowledge, but the experience of touching something far greater than your usual idea of yourself. I don’t want only to talk about the spiritual path, but to create the conditions in which each of you can explore it through your own experience.

A time for yourself. For the soul. For silence. For a deep spiritual experience.

Questions, Answered

Frequently Asked Questions

The things most people ask before booking an international retreat.

What's the cancellation and refund policy?

The $500 USD deposit per person is non-refundable once paid — it's what secures your spot and covers our upfront booking costs. The remaining balance is due 45 days before departure; if you cancel before that date, the balance you've paid (beyond the deposit) is refundable. After that 45-day window, the full payment becomes non-refundable, which is exactly why we recommend travel insurance that covers trip cancellation.

Do I need a visa to visit India?

Most travelers need a visa to enter India — many nationalities can apply for an e-Visa online in advance. Requirements vary by country, so please check your nearest Indian consulate or the official Indian e-Visa portal well before your trip, and apply early. This is your responsibility as a guest; we're happy to point you to resources but can't guarantee entry.

What fitness or yoga level do I need?

All levels are welcome, including complete beginners. Poses can be modified for every practice. The one thing to know: days start early (Hatha Yoga at 6:00 AM) and the schedule is full, so some stamina for long sitting and gentle movement helps — if you have any concerns, let us know and we'll talk them through.

I'm traveling solo — will I have a roommate?

Solo travelers are very welcome. The $2,000 package covers a shared three-person room at the Parmarth Niketan ashram, with fellow retreat guests. If you'd prefer a room of your own, a private single room at a hotel right next to the ashram is available for an additional $500 — please note it is outside the ashram grounds.

What should I pack?

Light, breathable layers for warm days and a warmer layer for cool mountain evenings; modest clothing for temple visits (shoulders and knees covered); comfortable shoes for walking around the ashram; and at least one outfit you genuinely don't mind ruining for Holi. We'll send a full packing list once you've booked.

What's the weather like in Rishikesh in March?

Generally mild and dry — warm, sunny days in the 70s–80s°F (low-to-mid 20s°C), and noticeably cooler mornings and evenings, especially by the river. It's a comfortable time of year to visit, but layers are still a good idea.
